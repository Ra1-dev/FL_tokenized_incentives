| [Witt, Heyer, Toyoda, Samek, Li (2023), Decentral and Incentivized Federated Learning Frameworks: A Systematic Literature Review](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.07855v3) | Decentralized incentive frameworks lack comparable implementation and evaluation evidence. | Systematic search and screening with a taxonomy of blockchain, incentive and learning choices. | Published federated-learning frameworks, rather than a newly trained benchmark. | Identifies incomplete performance evaluation and scalability analysis as recurring gaps. | Does not experimentally resolve the later cosine self-inclusion problem. | Motivates common-update comparisons and reporting computation and settlement costs together. |
| [Fraboni, Vidal, Lorenzi (2021), Free-rider Attacks on Model Aggregation in Federated Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/fraboni21a.html) | Clients can acquire the global model without supplying useful local training. | Analyzes unchanged-model and stochastically disguised updates under model averaging, including decaying perturbations. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and Shakespeare under FedAvg and FedProx. | Free-riders can obtain comparable final models while slowing convergence, especially at high participation fractions. | Does not measure token capture or implement stake penalties and vesting. | Separates model-access free-riding from token extraction; motivates adaptive-noise attacks as a limitation of fixed-noise experiments. |
| [Xu, Lyu, Ma, Miao, Foo, Low (2021), Gradient-Driven Rewards to Guarantee Fairness in Collaborative Machine Learning](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/8682cc30db9c025ecd3fee433f8ab54c-Abstract.html) | Contribution rewards should distinguish update quality without an auxiliary validation dataset. | Defines cosine coalition value, approximates its Shapley allocation, smooths contributions and rewards clients with differently sparsified global gradients. | MNIST, CIFAR-10, movie reviews and Stanford sentiment data. | Reports competitive predictive performance and contribution-sensitive gradient rewards. | Original rewards are gradients, not transferable tokens; an on-chain shadow allocation is an adaptation and cannot inherit its training guarantee automatically. | Extension rule and a test of self-inclusion under normalized, reputation-weighted references. |
| [Xu and Lyu (2021), A Reputation Mechanism Is All You Need: Collaborative Fairness and Adversarial Robustness in Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.10464v2) | Equal global-model access gives weak incentives and permits malicious contributions. | Uses normalized reputation-weighted aggregation, cosine-based moving reputation, permanent removal and gradient-download quotas. | Image and sentiment benchmarks, with free-riders and several poisoning transformations. | Reports strong accuracy and robustness in its tested settings; identifies difficulty separating similar honest and malicious gradients. | Does not evaluate exact token settlement or the false penalties caused by honest client-level DP noise. | Extension comparison; its removal and training feedback must be distinguished from our fixed-trajectory shadow adaptation. |
| [McMahan et al. | [Blanchard et al. (2017), Machine Learning with Adversaries: Byzantine Tolerant Gradient Descent](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/f4b9ec30ad9f68f89b29639786cb62ef-Abstract.html) | Arbitrary worker updates can defeat linear gradient aggregation. | Rank updates by squared distances to nearby peers; Multi-Krum averages a selected subset. | MNIST and Spambase; multilayer networks and convolutional experiments. | Neighbor-based selection tolerates tested Byzantine workers, with a speed/robustness tradeoff. | Convergence assumptions do not establish fair token allocation on heterogeneous local-training updates. | Acceptance-based payout baseline with the true fault budget supplied; no convergence guarantee claimed for our adaptation. |
| [Xu, Jiang, Niu, Jia, Li, and Poovendran (2024), ACE: A Model Poisoning Attack on Contribution Evaluation Methods in Federated Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/xu-zhangchen) | A client can appear valuable without supplying genuinely useful private data. | Predict future global updates using historical models and an L-BFGS approximation, with prediction-error filtering and optional amplification. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet under homogeneous and heterogeneous partitions. | Manipulates contribution rankings across multiple scorers while retaining strong global accuracy. | Does not validate our leave-one-out, EMA and on-chain settlement combination. | Bounds our claim: fixed noise/sign/label attacks do not establish robustness against adaptive contribution gaming. |
| [Bijun Wu and Oshani Seneviratne (2025), Blockchain-based Framework for Scalable and Incentivized Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14170) | Coordinate FL and incentivize contributions with blockchain transparency and scalable computation. | On-chain sample-weighted alignment scores, off-chain cumulative fairness checks and consistency multipliers. | Private-chain gas experiments across model-parameter sizes; no measured incentive-fairness experiment in the reported evaluation. | On-chain model computation becomes costly as dimension grows; the paper explicitly leaves incentive effectiveness unevaluated. | No controlled test of self-inclusion bias against the free-rider/poisoning suite or privacy-noise penalty comparison. | Closest alignment-and-gas predecessor. Our proposed contribution is the client-excluding reference and controlled common-update evaluation with exact settlement, not the introduction of blockchain rewards or slashing. |
| [Dong, Wang, Sun, Kampffmeyer, Knottenbelt, and Xing (2024), Defending Against Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning With Blockchain](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAI.2024.3376651) | Central aggregation and malicious clients threaten federated model quality. | Peer voting decides whether to accept a model; smart contracts redistribute proposer and voter stakes and exclude bankrupt clients. | Loan-default prediction on Lending Club, with label-poisoning proposers and malicious voters in a private-chain simulation. | Reported experiments preserve useful training and penalize malicious behavior under the stated voting assumptions. | Does not isolate the direct self-inclusion term in a cosine token scorer or compare the proposed client-excluding rule on identical updates. | Prior reward-and-slash defense. Our work tests a particular scoring correction and honest-client costs, rather than claiming slashing itself as novel. |
