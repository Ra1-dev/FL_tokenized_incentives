| [Fraboni, Vidal, Lorenzi (2021), Free-rider Attacks on Model Aggregation in Federated Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/fraboni21a.html) | Clients can acquire the global model without supplying useful local training. | Analyzes unchanged-model and stochastically disguised updates under model averaging, including decaying perturbations. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and Shakespeare under FedAvg and FedProx. | Free-riders can obtain comparable final models while slowing convergence, especially at high participation fractions. | Does not measure token capture or implement stake penalties and vesting. | Separates model-access free-riding from token extraction; motivates adaptive-noise attacks as a limitation of fixed-noise experiments. |
| [Xu, Lyu, Ma, Miao, Foo, Low (2021), Gradient-Driven Rewards to Guarantee Fairness in Collaborative Machine Learning](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/8682cc30db9c025ecd3fee433f8ab54c-Abstract.html) | Contribution rewards should distinguish update quality without an auxiliary validation dataset. | Defines cosine coalition value, approximates its Shapley allocation, smooths contributions and rewards clients with differently sparsified global gradients. | MNIST, CIFAR-10, movie reviews and Stanford sentiment data. | Reports competitive predictive performance and contribution-sensitive gradient rewards. | Original rewards are gradients, not transferable tokens; an on-chain shadow allocation is an adaptation and cannot inherit its training guarantee automatically. | Extension rule and a test of self-inclusion under normalized, reputation-weighted references. |
| [Xu and Lyu (2021), A Reputation Mechanism Is All You Need: Collaborative Fairness and Adversarial Robustness in Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.10464v2) | Equal global-model access gives weak incentives and permits malicious contributions. | Uses normalized reputation-weighted aggregation, cosine-based moving reputation, permanent removal and gradient-download quotas. | Image and sentiment benchmarks, with free-riders and several poisoning transformations. | Reports strong accuracy and robustness in its tested settings; identifies difficulty separating similar honest and malicious gradients. | Does not evaluate exact token settlement or the false penalties caused by honest client-level DP noise. | Extension comparison; its removal and training feedback must be distinguished from our fixed-trajectory shadow adaptation. |
| [McMahan et al. | [Blanchard et al. | [Xu, Jiang, Niu, Jia, Li, and Poovendran (2024), ACE: A Model Poisoning Attack on Contribution Evaluation Methods in Federated Learning](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/xu-zhangchen) | A client can appear valuable without supplying genuinely useful private data. | Predict future global updates using historical models and an L-BFGS approximation, with prediction-error filtering and optional amplification. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet under homogeneous and heterogeneous partitions. | Manipulates contribution rankings across multiple scorers while retaining strong global accuracy. | Does not validate our leave-one-out, EMA and on-chain settlement combination. | Bounds our claim: fixed noise/sign/label attacks do not establish robustness against adaptive contribution gaming. |
| [Bijun Wu and Oshani Seneviratne (2025), Blockchain-based Framework for Scalable and Incentivized Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14170) | Coordinate FL and incentivize contributions with blockchain transparency and scalable computation. | On-chain sample-weighted alignment scores, off-chain cumulative fairness checks and consistency multipliers. | Private-chain gas experiments across model-parameter sizes; no measured incentive-fairness experiment in the reported evaluation. | On-chain model computation becomes costly as dimension grows; the paper explicitly leaves incentive effectiveness unevaluated. | No controlled test of self-inclusion bias against the free-rider/poisoning suite or privacy-noise penalty comparison. | Closest alignment-and-gas predecessor. Our proposed contribution is the client-excluding reference and controlled common-update evaluation with exact settlement, not the introduction of blockchain rewards or slashing. |
| [Dong, Wang, Sun, Kampffmeyer, Knottenbelt, and Xing (2024), Defending Against Poisoning Attacks in Federated Learning With Blockchain](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAI.2024.3376651) | Central aggregation and malicious clients threaten federated model quality. | Peer voting decides whether to accept a model; smart contracts redistribute proposer and voter stakes and exclude bankrupt clients. | Loan-default prediction on Lending Club, with label-poisoning proposers and malicious voters in a private-chain simulation. | Reported experiments preserve useful training and penalize malicious behavior under the stated voting assumptions. | Does not isolate the direct self-inclusion term in a cosine token scorer or compare the proposed client-excluding rule on identical updates. | Prior reward-and-slash defense. Our work tests a particular scoring correction and honest-client costs, rather than claiming slashing itself as novel. |
| [Yin, Chen, Ramchandran, and Bartlett (2018), Byzantine-Robust Distributed Learning: Towards Optimal Statistical Rates](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/yin18a.html) | Arbitrary worker messages can corrupt distributed empirical-risk minimization. | Coordinate-wise median and trimmed-mean gradient aggregation, with statistical-rate bounds and a one-round median alternative. | MNIST logistic regression and CNN experiments under reversed or randomized labels, with equal random partitions. | Robust aggregation improves attacked accuracy in the reported experiments; guarantees depend on distributional and loss assumptions. | No heterogeneous local-training token fairness or client-excluding reward reference. | Motivates a robust reference building block without transferring its statistical guarantees to our non-IID reward heuristic. |
| [Cao, Fang, Liu, and Gong (2021), FLTrust: Byzantine-robust Federated Learning via Trust Bootstrapping](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/fltrust-byzantine-robust-federated-learning-via-trust-bootstrapping/) | Peer-only aggregation can be manipulated when no update is independently trusted. | Score updates by clipped cosine to a server update trained on clean root data; normalize update norms and aggregate by trust. | MNIST variants, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, colorectal histology images and human activity recognition, including adaptive attacks. | Reported attacks cause limited degradation when root data is representative; distribution mismatch is an explicit sensitivity. | Requires trusted training examples and measures model robustness rather than token reward fairness. | Shows that an independent reference is a distinct trust assumption. Our peer-based exclusion avoids root data but does not inherit this defense guarantee. |
| [Fung, Yoon, and Beschastnikh (2020), The Limitations of Federated Learning in Sybil Settings](https://www.usenix.org/conference/raid2020/presentation/fung) | Coordinated Sybils can overwhelm aggregation methods with bounded Byzantine assumptions. | FoolsGold lowers client learning rates using similarity of historical updates on indicative features, with pardoning and a logit transform. | MNIST, VGGFace2, KDDCup and Amazon text classification under Sybil poisoning. | Reduces coordinated attacks in tested settings; a single malicious client and similar honest data expose limitations. | No token settlement or direct analysis of a client appearing inside its own aggregate reward reference. | Distinguishes peer-to-peer similarity detection from aggregate-alignment rewards and motivates explicit honest false-penalty measurements. |
| [Beutel et al. (2022), Flower: A Friendly Federated Learning Research Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390) | Simulation and real-device FL research require a shared framework across heterogeneous hardware and software. | Separate orchestration from client computation, with resource-aware virtual clients and device-independent messages. | Amazon book ratings, FEMNIST, RealWorld activity recognition and CIFAR-10 device experiments. | Demonstrates large client populations and heterogeneous deployments; scheduling overhead depends on workload size. | Does not establish reward fairness or reproduce the later Message API's behavior and performance. | Framework provenance. Our pinned implementation and measured runtime remain necessary for the actual experiments. |
| [H. Brendan McMahan, Daniel Ramage, Kunal Talwar, and Li Zhang (2018), Learning Differentially Private Recurrent Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06963) | Language models can disclose a user's data even when raw training examples are not released. | Bound per-user updates, sample users independently and add Gaussian noise to their aggregate; compose privacy loss with a moments accountant. | Public Reddit posts grouped by user, with recurrent next-word models. | Large user cohorts permit useful privacy-accuracy tradeoffs at substantial additional computational cost. | Does not protect individually disclosed unnoised updates or test how reward scoring treats honest noised clients. | Distinguishes aggregate-release user privacy from our planned client-update release. Sampling, adjacency and the observer must match the accountant. |
| [Ilya Mironov (2017), Rényi Differential Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1109/CSF.2017.11) | Repeated heterogeneous private computations require composable privacy guarantees. | Track Rényi divergence over orders, add privacy curves under composition, and convert the result to an epsilon/delta bound. | Theoretical analysis and numerical comparisons of randomized-response, Laplace and Gaussian mechanisms. | Mechanism-specific curves can yield tighter accounting than generic composition bounds. | A privacy definition alone neither bounds an unclipped query nor supplies sampling amplification for an arbitrary disclosure process. | Basis for the planned accountant and for explicitly stating sensitivity, adjacency, disclosure and composition assumptions. |
| [Han Xiao, Kashif Rasul, and Roland Vollgraf (2017), Fashion-MNIST: a Novel Image Dataset for Benchmarking Machine Learning Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07747) | Digit classification is a limited test of generalization for new learning methods. | Construct a clothing-image benchmark with the same input format and split sizes as MNIST. | Zalando product photographs converted to grayscale, with silhouette labels and balanced classes. | The supplied classifiers generally perform worse on clothing images than on digits despite compatible input formats. | No federated partition, adversarial reward analysis or privacy guarantee. | A second task for the headline reward comparison while retaining the architecture; it does not establish broad real-world generalization. |
| [Ligeng Zhu, Zhijian Liu, and Song Han (2019), Deep Leakage from Gradients](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/60a6c4002cc7b29142def8871531281a-Abstract.html) | Sharing gradients can disclose the private examples that produced them. | Optimize dummy inputs and labels until their gradients match observed gradients for known model weights. | MNIST, CIFAR-100, SVHN, LFW and masked-language examples; modified smooth image models. | Demonstrates detailed reconstruction in tested settings; larger batches and sufficient perturbation make recovery harder. | Does not establish reconstruction success for our multi-epoch model differences or prove privacy from a failed attack. | Prevents treating local data retention, update hashes or noise without an accountant as a privacy guarantee. |
| [Baruch, Baruch, and Goldberg (2019), A Little Is Enough: Circumventing Defenses For Distributed Learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/ec1c59141046cd1866bbbcdfb6ae31d4-Abstract.html) | Outlier-rejection defenses may accept coordinated perturbations that stay within ordinary worker variation. | Estimate coordinate means and deviations from corrupted workers, then construct bounded coordinated shifts or backdoor updates. | MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 distributed learning with colluding non-omniscient workers. | Krum, trimmed mean and Bulyan can select harmful updates within the tested perturbation range. | Does not measure our token allocation or certify attacks against every heterogeneous FL reference. | Limits the interpretation of simple sign/noise tests and of a median reference: statistical proximity is not evidence of honest computation. |
| [Bonawitz et al. (2017), Practical Secure Aggregation for Privacy-Preserving Machine Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3133982) | A server should aggregate client vectors without learning individual contributions, despite client dropout. | Pairwise pseudorandom masks, threshold-shared recovery secrets and consistency checks reveal a sum under stated trust thresholds. | Java prototype with synthetic vectors, varying client pools and dropouts, plus wide-area execution. | Vector costs amortize communication; dropout recovery increases server work substantially. | Does not validate update quality or provide per-client scores from an aggregate-only interface. | Marks an explicit trust boundary: our scorer needs individual updates, so ordinary secure summation cannot be added without redesigning scoring. |
