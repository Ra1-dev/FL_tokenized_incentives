# Adaptive attackers

Generated by `python scripts/adaptive_assets.py` from the runs declared in `configs/adaptive_study.json`.

## Design

The main comparison uses fixed attacks. This study asks what happens when attackers adapt to similarity-based rewards. It reuses the unchanged server protocol, client training, aggregation, frozen Mstar scorer and published CGSV/RFFL parameters; `scripts/run_adaptive_study.py` replaces only the reported rows of attackers sampled in a round.

- **ALIE (full knowledge)** after Baruch et al.: every sampled attacker reports the coordinate-wise mean minus z standard deviations of the honest updates of the same round, with z from the published formula for n sampled clients and m sampled attackers. With one attacker in a round z = 0, so the attacker reports the honest mean. Knowing other clients' current updates is an upper bound: commit-reveal is designed to prevent exactly this.
- **Mimic (public information)**: a free-rider reports the previous round's aggregate update without training. It needs only public history, so commit-reveal does not prevent it.

Attackers are 10%, 20%, 30% of 30 clients; 5 seeds (42, 43, 44, 45, 46) with all 6 cells complete are analyzed (30 of 30 declared runs). Pooling averages the three fractions within each seed and resamples seeds.

## Findings

- **ALIE (full knowledge)**: pooled attacker share Mstar 52.62%, B1 34.24%, B2 27.26%, CGSV 34.64%, RFFL 35.16% against an equal-split share of 20.67% (average headcount 20.00%). Lowest: B0; highest: Mstar. Final model accuracy, identical for every rule: 98.72%.
- **Mimic (previous aggregate)**: pooled attacker share Mstar 47.90%, B1 22.90%, B2 27.26%, CGSV 35.11%, RFFL 36.32% against an equal-split share of 20.67% (average headcount 20.00%). Lowest: B0; highest: Mstar. Final model accuracy, identical for every rule: 98.78%.
- Honest low-reward rate under Mstar rises to 43.81% (ALIE) and 45.19% (mimic), against 15.00% and 24.73% for CGSV.
- Mechanism: the leave-one-out median sits near the centre of the honest updates. Its mean quality for attackers is 0.889 under ALIE and 0.562 under mimic, against 0.235 and 0.147 for honest non-IID clients in the same runs. Updates placed at the consensus therefore outscore genuine but heterogeneous training. All attackers in a round report the same vector, so the other attackers also pull an attacker's leave-one-out median toward it.
- Neither attack noticeably harms the model here: final accuracy is 98.72% and 98.78% against 98.84% without attackers (5 matching seeds of the main grid). The attacks steal rewards rather than damage training.

A share above the equal split means the rule pays the attackers more than their headcount; the rule then rewards the adaptive behavior instead of resisting it. The noise loophole closed by self-exclusion is replaced by a consensus-mimicry loophole that commit-reveal cannot prevent for the public-information attack.

## Scope

All rules score one common update matrix per round, so the training trajectory and accuracy are identical across rules within a run. No rule was tuned for these attacks, and both attacks are deliberately simple; stronger adaptive attacks remain possible. Runs: devices cuda; source commits d1c0b14.
